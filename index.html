01 — About

Biomedical engineering, then physics — because I wanted more.

I started my degree in Biomedical Engineering at the Universitat de Girona, ranking 1st in my class. A year in, I realized I wanted more breadth and depth — so I switched into Engineering Physics at UPC, where I'm now entering my third year.

What connects the two: I like understanding how and why things work, then testing that understanding against real data — whether that's a detector signal or a decay chain. In the summer of 2026 I took that curiosity to the University of York, working on two research projects at once.

Outside the lab, I sit on my university's student council, tutor high schoolers and early-university students in five subjects, and spent two years in technical retail — which taught me as much about explaining things clearly as any lecture did.

02 — Research & Projects

From particle detectors to cancer therapy.

01
Drift-field characterization of a μ-RWELL detector
TACTIC Collaboration — University of York, Summer 2026

Analyzed alpha-particle tracks in a gaseous μ-RWELL detector under varying drift-field conditions across two gas mixtures (Ar:CO2 and He:CO2), building a custom ROOT/C++ analysis pipeline to extract and compare timing and energy variables run by run.

ROOT / C++ Detector physics Statistical fitting
02
Ac-225 decay-chain analysis for targeted alpha therapy
University of York, Summer 2026

Evaluated chelator suitability for targeted alpha-particle cancer therapy by analyzing Ac-225 decay-chain data — correlation matrices, per-element activity curves, function fitting, and Crystal Ball fits.

Nuclear medicine Data analysis Correlation modeling
